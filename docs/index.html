1. What is new in this session, in one paragraph

The previous revision of this repository stated that no local instrument could rank detector fields — its own calibration measured ρ between −0.37 and +0.09 against live scores, and it called that "the whole problem". That is now fixed. We inverted the organizer's own metric against the 12 retrievable live-scored artifacts: per-dot kernel credit w against off-catalogue faults, fed through a saturating coverage model, ranks those 12 real scores at ρ = +0.923 in sample and +0.902 leave-one-out (RMSE 0.0364 / 0.0397). We then measured a second new quantity — the density of off-catalogue faults as a function of distance from the mapped catalogue — and used both to build and size a submission whose predicted score (0.3006) is above the family's best-ever 0.2778.

2. Measurement 1 — where off-catalogue faults actually are

Using USGS State Geologic Map Compilation (SGMC) faults that are not on the competition catalogue (79,615 px) as an independent compilation of off-catalogue faults, we measured fault density per unit area by distance from the nearest mapped fault. This is the quantity that should drive dot placement, and no prior submission in this project family used it.

Result. Off-catalogue faults are 3–4× enriched immediately beside a mapped trace, fall to baseline by ~600 m, and are less than half as dense beyond 10 km. The far field is a wasteland; the champion 0.2778 submission put ~31 % of its dots beyond 3 km and had no far-field suppression at all. Ours puts 2 of 30,000 dots beyond 5 km and 0 beyond 10 km.

Second measurement (independent support for using SGMC at all). Across the 12 live-scored historical artifacts — none of which used SGMC — the fraction of dots lying within 100 m of an off-catalogue SGMC fault correlates with the live score at Spearman ρ = +0.698, p = 0.012, n = 12. That is not circular: those artifacts never saw the layer.

Third measurement (the honest counterweight). Only 34.4 % of expert-mapped catalogue pixels lie within 300 m of any SGMC fault (footprint base rate 10.9 %, enrichment 3.16×). A geologic-map fault is a real but partial marker, which is why we learn the signature rather than emit along the traces.

3. Measurement 2 — an instrument that actually ranks live scores

Solving the organizer's metric algebraically for the true-positive term gives TP = S(0.2n + 0.8K)/(1 − 0.2S). Fitting a saturating coverage model TP = K(1 − e−a·n·w/K), S = TP/(0.2·TP + 0.2n + 0.8K) to the 12 retrievable live-scored artifacts gives K = 5,797 effective truth pixels and a = 1.169.

Promotion rule, and the honest caveat. Instrument A (catalogue-hidden spatial holdout) ranks mapped faults — precisely the pixels the organizer masks out of scoring — so its verdicts are reported but not allowed to veto. Instrument B is the only instrument here ever checked against organizer scores. H40-E was promoted on Instrument B (0.2891 at 40 k, the highest inside the 2× extrapolation guard); the two SGMC-tilted variants scored higher on Instrument B (0.4095, 0.4137) but were dropped by the extrapolation guard because 41–47 % of their dots sit within 100 m of an SGMC trace and their surrogate credit is therefore circular and inflated.

Two protocol deviations, disclosed. (1) H40-A was preregistered; H40-E/F/G were added after the first pass showed the pure geophysical field reaches w = 0.065, below every one of the 12 historical artifacts. The family-wise error rate across the whole candidate set is therefore not controlled at α = 0.05. (2) The holdout's evaluation domain was restricted mid-run to pixels outside the catalogue exclusion, because scoring fields on pixels a submission may not occupy credits them for skill they cannot convert into DTI. Both variants are computed and reported for every cell; on the unrestricted domain H40-E scores 0.5327 and the SPRT returns continue (6/12).

5. The sequential test, and why 12 cells

alpha = 0.05   beta = 0.10   p0 = 0.50   p1 = 0.70
upper = log(1/alpha) = +2.995732   -> accept H1, stop     (anytime-valid / Ville bound)
lower = log(beta)    = -2.302585   -> accept H0, stop
one win adds log(0.7/0.5) = +0.336472

9 wins are the arithmetic minimum to reach the upper boundary (⌈2.995732 / 0.336472⌉ = 9). The first pass of this run used 3×3 blocks and produced only 7 informative cells — a test that can never accept H1. That is a defect, not conservatism, and it is why the cell list is now 4×4 blocks × 2 draws, truncated at 12 informative cells. H40-E won the first 9 and the test stopped by its own rule with 3 cells unconsumed. Cells with fewer than 50 scorable hidden-truth pixels are excluded rather than booked as ties: recording a non-observation as a loss would be fabricating data.

6. Sizing the budget from the metric itself

Adding one predicted pixel changes the DW-Tversky denominator by exactly 0.2 whatever its quality, so a marginal dot pays iff its expected credit exceeds 0.2 × DTI. Applying that rule to the measured emission curve (independent of the fitted model's absolute level — it uses only the local slope):

Budget stepMarginal real creditBreak-even bar 0.2×DTIVerdict
15,000 → 20,0000.08200.0584pays
20,000 → 25,0000.07370.0600pays

40,000 → 45,0000.03550.0563does not pay

The rule selects 30,000 dots, which is also the peak of the fitted score curve (0.3006). Two independent derivations agreeing is the reason we ship it. The grid is capped at 45,000 because in the 12-artifact corpus the best live score achieved above 45,000 pixels is 0.2477, while the two best scores in the whole corpus (0.2778, 0.2708) sit at 37,654 and 40,199 pixels. An earlier maximin-over-scenarios rule was replaced: it is degenerate here, because the pessimistic scenario is monotone increasing in the budget and so always returns the largest grid value regardless of measured field quality.

A separate four-hypothesis portfolio was registered on 2026-10-05 (ranked hypotheses and frozen recipe, pre-score scoring-mask correction, training-only-label lock, preflight geometry lock). Only H39Y-01 was testable: a 2-pixel GeoDAWN ThK/UK/UTh ratio-gradient aligned with the competition cond_surf conductivity gradient. Eleven cells met the frozen eligibility rule; the sequential comparison stopped after the first five at its lower boundary — 0 wins, 5 losses, LLR −2.554128, accept_H0. Pooled proxy DTI 0.035772 vs 0.168755 for H39-A.

H39Y-01 is not promoted and must not be re-scored on those exposed cells. H39Y-02 and H39Y-03 remain untested and would need a fresh holdout or a family-wise α allocation; H39Y-04 is deferred pending verified source bytes. Its evaluator also hit a duplicate-key TypeError while assembling its post-stop report; the published report was reconstructed from the committed pre-score lock and saved per-tile outcomes, with no fold refit and no re-scoring (documented in the report).

10. Limitations

  • No hidden labels. Every field metric here is a proxy. The private truth is inaccessible.
  • The predicted score is an extrapolation. The instrument was fitted on w ∈ [0.0480, 0.1051]; this file measures 0.1461. The direction is supported (higher w has always meant a higher live score in the corpus) but the magnitude is not established.
  • Both instruments are compromised in opposite directions. Instrument A's truth (catalogue pixels) is the class of pixel the organizer masks out. Instrument B's truth (off-catalogue SGMC) is the layer the shipped detector was trained on, so its surrogate credit is an upper bound on real credit. Neither is the scored objective.
  • Portfolio-level error is not controlled. H40-E/F/G were added after the first pass; the SPRT controls sequential stopping for one declared comparison, not searching across candidates.
  • Owner-reported scores are not organizer receipts. No public artefact ties a raster hash to a score.
  • Provenance. Competition rasters come from owner-maintained GitHub mirrors, SHA-256 pinned. Hash agreement proves mirror integrity, not organizer authentication.
  • Public board ≠ final score. The Initial Prize Round scores a private chunk; the Final Prize Round re-scores the same file against an expanded label set built by experts reviewing every submission (verified staff statement). A 300 m dot spacing along coherent ridges is legible as fault traces, which matters there; a scattered isotropic field would not be.
  • AI disclosure is required by the competition rules and must be made in the submission narrative.
