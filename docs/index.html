1. Why the best-scoring submission scored what it scored

The reference point in the project brief is h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros at 0.2778, attributed by the project owner to the GEMSDOE32 site. Four things about the metric explain that number, and all four are measurable from bytes rather than guesswork:

(a) The DTI is a budget, not a segmentation score

The official metric, from the problem page, is DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw). Add one predicted pixel whose expected triangular-kernel credit is w: ΔTP = w, ΔFP = 1−w, ΔFN = −w, so the denominator changes by w + 0.2(1−w) − 0.8w = 0.2 — a constant, whatever the pixel's quality. The pixel pays for itself if and only if

w > 0.2 · DTI        (= 0.0556 at DTI = 0.2778)

That single line is the whole economics of this competition. GEMSDOE32 reached the same bar by two other routes (0.0548 measured empirically, 0.0520 as 0.2 × 0.26). We derive it directly from the published formula.

(b) Emitted mass dominates everything else

Across 30 re-readable historical artifacts carrying an owner-reported score, the Spearman rank correlation between emitted pixel count and live score is −0.686 (p < 1e-4):

The champion sits at the small-mass end of that ladder. Everything about our emission is built to stay there.

(c) Known faults are masked — their neighbourhood is not

DrivenData staff confirmed in community thread #11516 that known USGS/INGENIOUS fault pixels are excluded from evaluation. But the truth is the set of newly identified faults, and a pixel 200 m from a mapped fault is not masked — it is almost pure false positive. GEMSDOE32 measured the effect directly: deleting the 2,545 dots within 200 m of the catalogue moved 0.2708 → 0.2778, i.e. +0.0070 for removing 6 % of the pixels. We enforce a hard 2-pixel (200 m) catalogue exclusion at generation time, so the shipped file has 0 pixels on the catalogue and 0 within 200 m of it.

(d) The field itself still matters

The 0.2708 base raster is the most off-catalogue-oriented field in the family — 6.3 % of its dots fall within 200 m of a mapped fault, against 14.6 % for the d2.8 lineage — and it has the lowest catalogue-DTI of all 30 artifacts (0.0235). It is not being paid for re-predicting faults that are already mapped.

So: can we beat 0.2778?

Fit TP = A·nγ to the one clean within-lineage pair (60,069 px → 0.2477, 44,090 px → 0.2600) and impose the break-even condition γ·TP/n = 0.2·DTI:

The predicted gain is robust (+0.005 to +0.024) but the predicted budget is not, and the DTI surface is nearly flat across 13,000–45,000 px under every K (0.281–0.289 at n = 30,000). We therefore ship at 24,000 px: inside the flat region for every assumed truth size, below the break-even optimum implied by the organizer's own declared scale, and 36 % below the champion's pixel count.

The honest risk. At fixed budget the DTI is very nearly linear in true positives, so field quality is the first-order term and budget is second-order. Yet no local instrument we can build ranks detector fields. Against the 30 owner-reported live scores we measured Spearman ρ = −0.10 (catalogue-hidden spatially-blocked holdout), −0.24 (off-catalogue USGS SGMC), −0.37 raw / +0.09 budget-adjusted (catalogue DTI), and −0.08 (SGMC DTI). None is significant; the catalogue-DTI has the wrong sign because it rewards re-predicting mapped, masked faults. Our SPRT validates the field against hidden catalogue faults, which is a proxy for the real hidden set, not the real hidden set.

3. The selection test — declared before any fold was read

Informal peeking at a running holdout score makes the stopping decision depend on the data it judges, which inflates the chance of mistaking noise for improvement. We use a sequential probability ratio test instead:

H0: the candidate's per-cell win probability is ≤ p0 = 0.50
H1: it is ≥ p1 = 0.70          α = 0.05   β = 0.10
boundaries (anytime-valid / Ville):  upper = log(1/α) = +2.9957   lower = log(β) = −2.3026
cell outcome: candidate's mean percentile-rank lift on the hidden truth beats the control's (ties = losses)

Twelve independent cells are available (2 × 2 spatial blocks × 3 seeds), each hiding 25 % of catalogue connected components behind a 2-pixel collar. The log-likelihood ratio accumulates one cell at a time and the test stops at the first boundary crossed.

CandidateMean liftCells consumedLLRDecision

H39-A-model0.66989 of 12+3.028accept H1 stopped at cell 9
H39-C-joint0.64469 of 12+3.028accept H1

H39-B-model0.58879 of 12+3.028accept H1

Promotion rule, pre-declared: ship the SPRT-accepted candidate with the highest holdout lift. That is H39-A. The test used 9 cells, not 12, because it crossed its own upper boundary and stopped — we did not keep going to make the number look better, and all five results are reported, not just the winner.

Limitation. The SPRT controls the sequential-stopping error for one pre-declared pairwise comparison. It does not correct for searching a portfolio of candidates; that is handled by registering all five with their layers and rationale before any of them was scored. Fold independence is an explicit assumption of the test, and a spatial collar reduces but does not prove it.

4. How the file was built

  1. Restore. Competition rasters pulled from SHA-256-pinned owner mirrors (scripts/restore_data.py) because the official data page redirects to login. Hash agreement proves mirror integrity, not organizer authentication.
  2. Stack. 19 supplied bands + 21 derived transforms = a 40-field uint8 cube held in RAM (95 MB, deliberately not float32 — a float32 design matrix was OOM-killed on this 3 GB box).
  3. Fit. Gradient-boosted trees under 2 × 2 spatial blocking, 3 seeds averaged, on two targets: catalogue faults with hard negatives (channel A) and off-catalogue SGMC faults (channel B). Out-of-fold AUC: A 0.661 (0.588–0.707), B 0.757 (0.701–0.803). The catalogue-distance field is excluded from the design by construction.
  4. Select. SPRT as above → H39-A.
  5. Emit. Best-first Poisson-disk thinning, minimum separation 2.8 px, hard 2 px catalogue exclusion, 24,000 dots. Median nearest-neighbour distance 3.00 px (mean 3.64, p10 2.83, p90 4.47).
  6. Write and audit. Two encodings, both re-opened from disk and checked cell by cell (scripts/validate_submission.py): 15/15 for the all-finite file, 14/14 for the NaN-outside twin.
  7. Uniqueness gate. Compared against 241 retrievable same-grid rasters from the sibling GEMSDOE repositories (scripts/verify_unique.py, receipt registry/uniqueness.json).

Irregularities flagged for review

  • IR-39-01 — the sample submission is not what the page says. The mirrored sample_submission.tif is described as "total fault absence" but contains 60,988 pixels equal to 1.0, coinciding exactly with labels.tif == 1. We use it only as a grid template; its footprint (5,167,373 px) is independently confirmed by labels.tif, so the geometry is safe even though the file's documented semantics are not.
  • IR-39-02 — existing_faults.tif and labels.tif are byte-identical (sha256 7ba308cc…). We treat labels.tif as the single authoritative catalogue.
  • IR-39-03 — instrument discordance. GEMSDOE32 reports the SGMC instrument at ρ = +0.54 (n = 12); we measure −0.080 (n = 30). Calibration sets differ; neither is significant.
  • IR-39-04 — the manifest serialiser dropped nested per-cell tables. The previous revision's filter kept only keys named dti/tp/fp/fn/n_truth, and fold names are not among them, so the per-cell tables were written empty. The current manifest is schema_version: 2 and stores the full table.
  • IR-39-05 — the 0.2778 attribution is unverified. The brief attributes 0.2778 to h33-h33-2-b2-…-e5eb6e7e-zeros, but the GEMSDOE32 site marks that file "UNSCORED", and the public leaderboard shows a 0.2778 row without a filename. We treat 0.2778 as an official board row, not a verified score for that specific file.
  • IR-39-06 — the brief's rules link is misspelled. It prints docs.nlr.gov; the live host is docs.nrel.gov. Both currently resolve to the same document.

7. What still needs doing, and what is in the way

  1. Build an instrument that actually ranks fields. This is the single highest-value next step: every proxy we can compute locally has ~zero rank correlation with live scores. Candidates: MINE-based conditional information on held-out spatial folds; a generative "live-mirror" model of the hidden set calibrated on all 30 scored artifacts with cross-validated error bars; or treating the three weekly submission slots as a designed experiment rather than three lottery tickets.
  2. Resolve the greedy-packing disagreement. GEMSDOE32 measured greedy max-coverage emission on the scatter-smoothed field at +0.0247 on their live-anchored model but −0.0033 on the raw surface. The two instruments disagree in sign; settle it with the better instrument from (1) before shipping it.
  3. Unblock H39-X by obtaining the official GeoDAWN Area-2 raw CSVs, or formally close the hypothesis.
  4. Integrate external layers not yet used at native resolution: the USGS FDSN earthquake catalogue (the supplied ieq_n100a15 band was measured at autocorrelation 0.9986 at 1 km — it carries no fault-scale information), InSAR/geodetic strain-rate grids, and the 1 m 3DEP DEM beyond the derived scarp layer.

8. Limitations

  • No hidden labels. Only DrivenData's private holdout is scored, and it is not accessible. Every field metric here is a proxy measured against faults that are not the scored set.
  • The public board is not the prize. The public leaderboard scores a public chunk of the new faults; the Initial Prize Round scores a private chunk; the Final Prize Round re-scores the same file against an expanded label set that includes faults experts verify after reading everyone's submissions. A file tuned hard to the public number is not automatically tuned to the prize.
  • Owner-reported scores are not organizer receipts. No public artefact ties a raster hash to a score. The board is dynamic and authoritative; a 2026-10-05 reading showed #1 at 0.3262, above the 0.3195 quoted in the brief.
  • Provenance. Competition inputs come from owner-maintained GitHub mirrors, SHA-256 pinned. That proves mirror integrity, not organizer authentication.
  • Compute. This box has 3 GB RAM and 2 cores, which is why the feature cube is uint8 and the emission uses a spatial-hash thinning pass rather than a full distance matrix.
  • AI disclosure is required by the competition rules and must be included in the submission narrative.
